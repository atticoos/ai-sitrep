US Treasury chief says Washington has mapped Iran's networks and financial channels used to evade sanctions.

[Reuters-syndicated factbox on the Operation Economic Outcast rollout of August 24.]

Dubbed Operation Economic Outcast, the campaign expands sanctions and secondary-sanctions risks across key sectors of Iran's economy while targeting companies, individuals, vessels and intermediaries involved in oil revenues, military procurement, cyber operations and sanctions evasion.

Bessent, quoting his own X post: "In the Second World War, D-Day marked the historic beginning of a campaign with our allies to target and drive the enemy from its positions… Today, in that same spirit, we are launching an economic onslaught against Iran's financial connections…"

Speaking at a news conference Monday, Bessent said the initiative, directed by President Trump, represents a shift from containing Iran to attempting to fundamentally undermine its economic ability to operate internationally. "Our objective is to sever every economic lifeline that sustains this tyrannical regime until Tehran stands alone."

The new campaign expands the scope of potential secondary sanctions to five sectors: digital assets, technology, gold, aviation and shipping. Treasury said it had sanctioned nearly 60 entities, individuals and vessels across multiple jurisdictions, targeting networks allegedly involved in illicit nuclear and missile technology procurement, cyber operations and the generation and movement of oil revenues.

Bessent described the approach as a "zero-leakage" policy. "Beginning today, the actions of Treasury and other agencies will tighten the noose and block every potential source of revenue that funds the IRGC and the evil Iranian regime."

Treasury said one of its targets is a network of companies and "shadow fleet" vessels operating in locations including the UAE, Hong Kong, China, Singapore, Switzerland, and Europe to transport Iranian oil and channel revenue to the IRGC-Quds Force.

"Any entity that facilitates money laundering on behalf of Iran will be removed from the US dollar system. The clock just started ticking," Bessent said. He also said he expected a major financial institution to be sanctioned by the end of the week, without identifying the institution.

On timelines: "We are giving everyone the opportunity to remedy bad behavior… We do not have infinite patience here." Every country would receive a defined timeline to shut down identified Iran-related activities; Washington would act unilaterally on non-compliance. "We're not going to name names. We've already seen some results."

Bessent framed the choice for Iran as "complete global isolation and a subsistence economy or a path back to normalcy with an opportunity to rejoin the global economy." Treasury emphasized "the ultimate goal of sanctions is not to punish, but to bring about a positive change in behavior."
