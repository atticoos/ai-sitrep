By CNBCTV18.com   August 25, 2026, 7:25:36 AM IST (Published)
2 Min Read
Oil prices extended their decline on Tuesday August 25, as Washington intensified economic pressure on Iran and countries trading with it, in an effort to force open the Strait of Hormuz.
Brent crude hovered near $92 a barrel, building on a drop of more than 2% in the previous session. West Texas Intermediate traded close to $85.
The retreat followed remarks from US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who said on Monday that nations would be given a defined timeline to cut ties with Iran or face unilateral penalties. He described the initiative as an "economic D-Day" campaign aimed at isolating Tehran from the global economy.

The Office of Foreign Assets Control moved in parallel, sanctioning roughly 60 entities linked to Iran&#x27;s oil-revenue operations, including vessels from its shadow fleet used to move petroleum products, the Treasury said in a statement.
Iran&#x27;s Economy Minister Seyed Ali Madanizadeh pushed back against the pressure campaign, telling Iranian state television that the country stood fully prepared for the fresh measures.
Iranian citizens were reported to be facing worsening fuel shortages and longer queues at petrol stations as the sanctions took a toll on the domestic economy, according to Bloomberg.
Prices fell even as security threats around key shipping routes persisted. The British Navy reported that a tanker had been struck and disabled by a projectile near Ash Shishah in Oman, within the Strait of Hormuz.
Separately, Houthi militants backed by Iran claimed to have targeted a Saudi Arabian supertanker transiting the Red Sea.

Meanwhile, a separate trend was adding strain to global supply dynamics. Asian refiners were on track to nearly double their purchases of US crude for September loading, according to tracking firms Kpler, Vortexa and Sparta Commodities, with trade estimates rising to more than 40 million barrels from 22 million barrels in August, Bloomberg reported.
The conflict, now in its sixth month, has continued to disrupt crude and refined fuel shipments from West Asia and has driven crude prices up more than 50% this year.
