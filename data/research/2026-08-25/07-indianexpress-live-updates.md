(Photo: AP) The United States has launched a sweeping new round of sanctions against Iran, aimed at cutting off all financial lifelines to Tehran’s government. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confirmed the move, and said President Trump is pushing US allies to back the measures too.
Iran has responded defiantly, with officials warning of serious consequences if Washington keeps up the pressure  including threats to shut down Gulf oil exports and warnings that any country siding with the sanctions could be treated as taking part in an act of war. Meanwhile, the Pentagon has signalled that military options remain firmly on the table.

US officials say the new sanctions target five key sectors  digital assets, technology, gold, aviation and shipping  along with Iran’s oil revenue and roughly 60 entities, vessels and individuals accused of propping up the regime.
Iranian government spokeswoman Fatemeh Mohajerani said the country’s leadership would guide Iran through the coming “economic hardships.”
Iran’s security chief, Mohsen Rezaei, warned that Tehran could halt all Gulf oil exports if the US economic pressure campaign continues.
Rezaei added that any country backing Washington’s sanctions would be treated as taking part in an act of war against Iran.
US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said Washington has not ruled out military strikes against Iran, in the Strait of Hormuz or elsewhere in the region.

US senator slams new sanctions as ‘desperate window dressing’
US Senator Chris Murphy has slammed the White House’s new sanctions on Tehran, calling them “desperate window dressing” to cover up Washington’s failure in its military war on Iran.
“‘D-Day’ is desperate window dressing. Iran is winning the war. Trump is losing. And Americans are paying a huge, huge cost,” Murphy said on X.
He documented a series of US failures in the conflict.
“Iran didn’t control the Strait before the war. Now they do,” Murphy wrote.
He said Iran has effectively won the military portion of the war, pointing out that Tehran retains 30 of its 33 missile sites along the Strait of Hormuz, while the US “fired everything we have and now we’ve run out”.
Iran has “all the leverage now”, Murphy added, arguing that the new sanctions will not change Tehran’s negotiating position.
“We’ve already sanctioned basically everything in Iran we can. There’s little left. ‘D-Day’ is bluster,” he wrote.
“The war is basically over. Tragically, Iran won. This is all window dressing,” Murphy added.
