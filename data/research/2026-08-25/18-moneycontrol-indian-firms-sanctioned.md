US has sanctioned four India-based companies and individuals as part of its fresh economic pressure campaign against Iran called "Operation Economic Outcast".

The companies were sanctioned for their alleged involvement in Iran-related petroleum and petrochemical trade, with US accusing them of facilitating imports from Iranian-origin suppliers.

Among those named is customs broker Portease Partners LLP, which allegedly "facilitated the import of multiple shipments of Iranian petrochemical products to India", according to the US State Department. The State Department also designated two of the firm's partners, Indrismiya Ashrafmiya Shekh and Harish Ramchandra Rangi, both Indian nationals.

Sadashiva Overseas Ltd, alleging that the India-based company imported around $69 million worth of Iranian-origin petroleum products from multiple companies, including US-designated Bonjoure Commodity F.Z.E., between February 2024 and June 2025. "SADASHIVA OVERSEAS is being designated pursuant to section 3(a)(ii) of E.O. 13846 for knowingly engaging in a significant transaction for the purchase, acquisition, sale, transport, or marketing of petroleum or petroleum products from Iran," the State Department said.

Another Indian company named in the sanctions was PP Softtech Private Ltd. The US said the company imported approximately $25 million worth of Iranian-origin petroleum products between January 2024 and June 2025. The State Department also designated Prashant Garg, an Indian national and director of PP Softtech.

The fourth company, Prakrutees Infra Impex India Private Ltd, was accused by Washington of importing Iranian-origin petroleum products worth approximately $25 million from multiple companies, including Bonjoure Commodity F.Z.E., between May 2023 and February 2026.

[Context paragraphs] US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Monday announced the wider economic pressure campaign against Iran, warning that entities helping Tehran launder money could be cut off from the US dollar system. "Any entity that facilitates money laundering on behalf of Iran will be removed from the US dollar system. The clock just started ticking," Bessent told reporters. "Around the globe, our objective is to sever every economic lifeline that sustains this tyrannical regime… this is economic asphyxiation of this regime." The Treasury has also expanded the scope of potential secondary sanctions to five sectors — digital assets, technology, gold, aviation and shipping. Bessent warned the measures could extend to financial institutions globally, including Chinese banks: "No one is above the reach of US sanctions."
