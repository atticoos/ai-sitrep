TOI live blog, Aug 26 (search-result pubDate Wed, 26 Aug 2026 15:58 GMT). Trimmed of navigation and non-Iran regional items.

BLOG SUMMARY:
Iran and Oman have agreed to work on a temporary maritime corridor through the Strait of Hormuz and a joint mine-clearing operation, but Tehran has made clear that this does not constitute a reopening of the waterway. Only commercial vessels would be permitted under the proposed arrangement, while military ships would remain excluded. Qatar has voiced support for the negotiations, while the US has begun restoring personnel at some Middle Eastern diplomatic missions.

REZAEI — CENTRAL CHANNEL IF US COMPLIES:
Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said Iran has agreed to a corridor with Oman, part of which lies in Omani waters and part in Iranian waters. Rezaei said ships will transit through a designated central channel in the Strait of Hormuz if the United States meets Iran's conditions.

GHARIBABADI — MILITARILY CLOSED; ARMED FORCES LEAD THE NEGOTIATION:
Iranian deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi told state television late Tuesday that commercial shipping traffic into the Persian Gulf would pass through Iranian waters, while the outbound route would pass partly through Iranian waters and partly through Oman's waters. The routes would be temporary, and Iran and Oman must discuss a permanent maritime traffic plan within 30 to 60 days. Trump has threatened to bomb Oman if it "gets in the way," as his administration opposes parts of the evolving deal. "If the understanding becomes binding, no military vessel will be permitted to pass through the Strait of Hormuz." "Militarily, the Strait of Hormuz is closed." Iran's armed forces are playing the leading role in determining the new maritime routes: "The main guidance regarding the routes actually lies with military friends and the armed forces complex, and the General Staff of the Armed Forces and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy are at the forefront of it."

IRGC — NOT REOPEN WITHOUT US ACCEPTING CONDITIONS:
"If the United States stops obstructing and returns to the memorandum, we can reopen the Strait of Hormuz within the framework of the agreement… If the United States does not accept our conditions, the Strait of Hormuz will not be opened under any circumstances." He claimed Iran controlled passage through the waterway and that opposing warships remained at least 400 km away.

AGREEMENT NOT FINALISED (Wednesday, citing Reuters senior Iranian source):
Iran and Oman were still working on the details of the proposed agreement; earlier, an IRGC spokesperson said the two had reached understandings on their respective shares of the strait's waters and revenues.

SAUDI SUPERTANKERS VIA STS OFF OMAN (Wednesday, shipping data):
Two supertankers carrying 4 million barrels of Saudi crude are bound for China after loading the cargoes via ship-to-ship transfers off Oman.

FIVE TANKERS TRANSIT IN 24 HOURS (Kpler, 0408 GMT Wednesday):
Two tankers carrying LPG and one carrying bitumen exited the Gulf via the strait; two empty product tankers entered from the Gulf of Oman. On Monday, four commodity vessels transited.

TRUMP TRUTH SOCIAL — MINES:
Trump said the US Navy had informed him that all mines had been removed or detonated from international waters in the Strait of Hormuz, warning that any vessel involved in placing new mines in international waters would be "immediately and systematically destroyed".

QATAR — MINE-CLEARING TALKS:
The talks also covered a proposed joint project to clear mines from the strait, according to Qatar's foreign ministry.

SECRET SERVICE ON BARRON TRUMP VIDEO:
The US Secret Service confirmed it is aware that Iranian state media aired a video that appears to threaten the life of Barron Trump, Trump's youngest son.

CANADA — EX-IRAN MINISTRY OFFICIAL ALLEGED TO HAVE AIDED IRGC FINANCING:
Canadian government documents released in redacted form by the Immigration and Refugee Board allege Abbas Omidi — a deputy director-general in Iran's Ministry of Industry, Mines and Trade who moved to Canada in 2022 — used his senior position and influence in ways that benefited Iran's ability to generate mining revenue and finance the IRGC, military and weapons production.

INDIA — OPERATION SANKALP:
Indian Navy warships are in the Gulf of Oman for Operation Sankalp and conducting anti-piracy operations in the Gulf of Aden, helping Indian merchant vessels reach their destinations safely.
