TRUMP, "VERY BIG VICTORY" (Wednesday):
President Trump told Al Jazeera on Wednesday that the U.S. was "achieving a very big victory" against Iran, but that the war "will continue for as long as necessary" and there's no timetable to resume peace talks with Tehran. Speaking to conservative radio host Glenn Beck, Trump said he doesn't "think" Iranian Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei is dead, and if he is, the Iranians are putting on a pretty good "show." Just less than a week ago, Trump said there were no ongoing talks between the U.S. and Iran and none scheduled, but Iran has kept up a dialogue with Oman aimed at establishing a proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and to reinstitute a ceasefire with the U.S. The White House says there are currently no direct talks underway; Iran has indicated this week plans to propose a temporary measure, in conjunction with Oman, to reopen the strait to commercial shipping traffic.

IRGC SPOKESMAN WEDNESDAY — DEAL REACHED WITH OMAN, BUT U.S. MUST ACCEPT CONDITIONS:
The spokesman for the IRGC said Wednesday that Iranian and Omani officials reached an agreement weeks ago on terms under which the two nations could control shipping traffic through the Strait of Hormuz — and share the proceeds from fees incurred — but he indicated the U.S. had failed to accept the arrangements. "The Strait of Hormuz belongs to Iran and the Sultanate of Oman… About a month ago, we entered into negotiations with Oman and reached results that were acceptable to both sides," Brig. Gen. Hossein Mohebbi said, according to Iran's state broadcaster IRIB. "In these negotiations, agreements had been reached regarding the share of each of the two countries from the waters of the strait and the share of Iran and Oman from its revenues." "The Strait of Hormuz is under our control, and no enemy military vessel is present inside the Persian Gulf," Mohebbi insisted Wednesday, claiming all U.S. warships had moved at least 250 miles from the strait. Iran and Oman said Tuesday the two nations continue discussing a proposal to reopen the strait by way of a temporary shipping route, as well as a plan to clear sea mines, but no new agreement has been reached.

GHARIBABADI WEDNESDAY — REOPENING DEPENDS ON U.S. ENDING THE WAR:
Kazem Gharibabadi told Iranian state broadcaster IRIB "the blockade is not the only quid pro quo for the Strait of Hormuz." Iran and Oman said in a joint statement Tuesday that they were discussing a proposal for a temporary route allowing ships to transit the strait, and a plan to remove sea mines.

OMAN'S FOREIGN MINISTER TUESDAY — 'HOPEFUL' CORRIDOR ANNOUNCED 'SOON':
"I am hopeful we will soon announce a temporary corridor for the Strait of Hormuz and practical arrangements to restore safe navigation," Oman's Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi said in a social media post. "Future management of the Strait and a permanent solution will follow in due course." However, Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi warned on Wednesday the United States would have to lift its blockade on the waterway.

OIL:
Both main crude oil contracts have tumbled this week as Iran and Oman say they're edging toward a deal to open a temporary shipping corridor in the Strait of Hormuz.
