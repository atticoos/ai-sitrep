The G20 finance ministers failed to agree on a joint statement after China objected to several provisions, including language addressing huge trade surpluses and countries' reliance on exports for growth.

**Why it matters:** China was the lone holdout, with the other 19 members backing the language — a sign of widening concern over the pressure that Chinese exports are putting on industries around the world.

**The backdrop:** The joint statements are meant to spell out where the world's largest economies agree on shared economic problems and how to address them.

- Several G20 finance meetings since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine have failed to produce communiqués, as geopolitical divisions blocked consensus.

**Between the lines:** In place of a joint statement, the U.S. issued a chair's statement — as is the tradition for the host country — noting that the language was agreed to by all members except China.

- Treasury said China objected to four sections: one addressing the Strait of Hormuz and ongoing wars and conflicts; two focused on global imbalances, including countries' reliance on exports and greater IMF scrutiny, and one on sovereign debt restructurings.

**The intrigue:** Earlier on Tuesday, other global finance officials had signaled that Chinese officials were balking at communiqué language over global economic imbalances, a key theme of the gathering.

**What they're saying:** "I do think it is incredible to get 19 countries to agree to anything," Bessent told reporters at a press conference in Asheville, North Carolina, at the conclusion of the meetings.

- "We believe that the non-market-based economies pushing out a never-ending stream of cheap exports is not sustainable," Bessent said, referring to China. "The fact that 19 countries did want to address this shows the sheer enormity of the problem."

**Of note:** The statement does not mention Russia's invasion of Ukraine or the Middle East conflict by name, despite those conflicts looming large over the gathering.

- It refers broadly to "ongoing wars and conflicts."
