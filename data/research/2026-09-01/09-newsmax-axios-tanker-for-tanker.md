The United States on Tuesday struck two Iranian government tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, opening a new and potentially dangerous "tanker-for-tanker" phase in the escalating confrontation with Tehran, according to Axios.

The strikes were part of a broader U.S. military operation against roughly 100 Iranian targets and represented a significant shift in American strategy, with U.S. officials telling Axios that the tanker attacks were carried out in direct retaliation for Iranian aggression against commercial shipping and U.S. forces.

The Iranian tankers were anchored off Iran's coast north of the U.S. naval blockade line when American forces struck their engine rooms.

The operation came after two supertankers carrying Saudi crude were attacked while transiting the Strait of Hormuz, with the vessels struck within minutes of each other by unidentified projectiles.

Newsmax, citing shipping intelligence and vessel-tracking firms, reported that the Saudi-flagged Sidr and the Liberian-flagged Senegal Prosperity were carrying Saudi oil when they were attacked.

No group immediately claimed responsibility.

The attacks came as the United States and Iran resumed direct military exchanges after a month-long lull, with Washington accusing Tehran of threatening commercial shipping and American forces across the region.

The United States launched strikes against Iranian air-defense systems, radar installations, and maritime assets around the Strait of Hormuz, while also targeting capabilities that Washington said Iran could use to threaten ships, according to the Associated Press.

Iran responded with ballistic missiles and drones directed at U.S. positions in Jordan, Bahrain, and Kuwait, according to U.S. officials cited by the Associated Press and Axios.

The latest exchange followed Iranian attempts to lay mines in the Strait of Hormuz and missile attacks directed toward U.S. military positions, according to the Trump administration.

President Donald Trump warned that Iran would face a harsher response if it retaliated against the latest American strikes.

Trump said Iran had been attempting to rebuild radar and other military capabilities and warned that the United States was prepared to strike again if Tehran continued attacking American forces or commercial shipping.

The administration has made control of the Strait of Hormuz a central objective of its military campaign because the waterway is one of the world's most important energy chokepoints.

The United States has spent weeks working to reopen a shipping corridor through the southern portion of the strait, allowing tankers to move oil out of the Persian Gulf despite the conflict.

Axios previously reported that U.S. forces had been moving roughly 15 to 20 tankers through the corridor on many nights, with U.S. officials estimating that about 10 million barrels of oil a day were being transported through the waterway — roughly half the prewar volume.

But the latest attacks threaten that progress.

Reuters reported that commercial traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remained sharply depressed, with preliminary Kpler data showing only five vessels transiting the waterway on Monday and no liquid tankers among them.

The renewed fighting is already putting additional pressure on global energy markets.

Brent crude rose above $92 a barrel after the tanker attacks, according to the Wall Street Journal, while the broader escalation has raised fresh concerns about whether the United States can keep the strategic waterway open.

The stakes are particularly high because roughly one-fifth of the world's oil supply normally moves through the Strait of Hormuz.

The latest U.S. response also establishes a potentially important precedent.

Under the new approach described by Axios, Iran's attacks on commercial vessels can trigger direct American attacks on Iranian government vessels, meaning future attacks on tankers could produce another round of retaliatory strikes against Iranian shipping.

The immediate military exchange is already broader than a fight over individual vessels.

Iran has launched missiles and drones toward American positions in several countries, while the United States has expanded its strikes against Iranian military infrastructure around the Strait of Hormuz and elsewhere in southern Iran.
