Qatar's Big Diplomacy Push In Tehran, With An Eye On Hormuz | Times Now

Qatar's PM Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani met Iranian officials in Tehran, discussing de-escalation, an Iran-Oman plan for safe Hormuz passage, mine-clearing, and respecting navigation freedom and regional sovereignty.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi with Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani | File Photo

Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani held talks with Iranian officials in Tehran on Thursday in a bid to revive diplomacy to resolve the Iran war. The visit comes as the US-Israel-Iran war, that began on February 28, nears its sixth month. While the fighting has largely been paused, there is no diplomatic breakthrough in sight.

The Qatari PM, who is also the country's foreign minister, discussed a proposed plan between Iran and Oman to allow ships safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz and clear the vital commercial waterway of dangerous mines.

Recently, Iran and Oman reached a revenue-sharing agreement . Tehran has kept the Strait shut after the beginning of the war and has also cautioned that a re-opening of the strait will take more than a deal with Oman.

Al Thani’s office said the prime minister emphasised the "necessity of respecting the sovereignty of neighbouring countries and the freedom of navigation" on the strait.

Qatar's foreign ministry said in a post on X that Al Thani and Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi discussed "the efforts being made to reduce escalation." The two exchanged views on the current developments in the region and efforts to reduce tensions and create suitable conditions for resuming dialogue, Iranian news agencies said.

Al Thani also met Thursday with Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian and parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf to review efforts to reduce escalation and resume dialogue, the Qatari government said.

Earlier today, White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt said that US President Donald Trump "continues to retain all options on the table" with regard to Iran.

"No negotiations are happening right now, and this will continue until the president feels that maybe they come to the table in a meaningful way...We've had Operation Epic Fury to destroy their military. Now we have Operation Economic ‌Outcast to ⁠destroy their economy," Leavitt said in an interview with Fox News.

The US and Iran had reached a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) in June this year to end the conflict, however, it soon collapsed due to differences over the Strait of Hormuz, from where one-fifth of the world's oil supplies passed before the war began on February 28.

Tehran has said the strait will remain shut unless the US meets Iranian conditions agreed under the June interim ceasefire. Those conditions include an end to the US blockade on Iranian ports, compensation and removal of sanctions.
