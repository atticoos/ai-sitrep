Rezaei says Tehran is preparing conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz

Iran is preparing a list of conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz after mediators asked Tehran to outline its demands, Iranian security official Mohsen Rezaei said.

Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said ending the regional war was among the conditions being considered for reopening the strategically important waterway.

He said Iran had also reached an agreement with Oman on a shipping corridor through the strait, with parts of the route passing through Omani waters and others through Iranian waters.

According to Rezaei, ships would use a designated central channel if the United States met Iran's conditions.

Rezaei's remarks came after mediators asked Tehran to clarify what would be required for the Strait of Hormuz to reopen.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards have previously said the waterway would not reopen unless Washington lifts what Tehran describes as a blockade of Iranian ports, pays compensation and removes sanctions.

A senior source also said Iran and Oman were still working on details of an agreement concerning the waterway after the Revolutionary Guards said the two countries had agreed on arrangements for sharing control of the strait and its revenues.

The Strait of Hormuz is one of the world's most important energy routes.

Before the war began in February, roughly one-fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas shipments passed through the waterway.

Shipping through the strait has largely stopped since the conflict began.

Ceasefire agreements announced by Washington and Tehran in April and June were intended to help restore maritime traffic, but both arrangements failed to hold.

Rezaei said Tehran was now preparing its conditions in response to requests from mediators, while stressing that the United States would need to take practical steps before Iran agrees to reopen the waterway.
