Qatar PM-FM meets Iranian Foreign Minister in Tehran to discuss mediation and Strait of Hormuz de-escalation

Qatar’s prime minister meets Iran’s foreign minister in Tehran to discuss a temporary Strait of Hormuz shipping corridor, mine-clearing efforts and regional de-escalation

Qatar’s Prime Minister and Minister of Foreign Affairs Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani met with Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi in Tehran on Thursday to discuss regional de-escalation and efforts to reopen the Strait of Hormuz.

During the talks, the two officials reviewed the latest regional developments and efforts to lower tensions and create conditions conducive to dialogue, aiming to bolster regional security and stability.

According to a statement published by the Qatari Foreign Ministry, discussions focused heavily on a proposed interim framework that includes establishing a temporary joint shipping corridor through the Strait of Hormuz and an agreement to implement a joint project to clear the strait of mines.

The Qatari prime minister stressed the necessity of respecting the sovereignty of neighbouring countries and freedom of navigation, emphasising the importance of continuing diplomatic efforts to de-escalate tensions and resolve disputes through dialogue.

Araghchi expressed his appreciation for Qatar’s ongoing diplomatic initiatives and efforts to reduce escalation and support open channels of communication.

The meetings mark the Qatari top diplomat’s first visit to Iran since the U.S.-Iran war erupted on February 28.

While a memorandum of understanding was signed in June through Qatari and Pakistani mediation, its implementation fell apart over disagreements over control of the Strait of Hormuz and the lifting of blockades.

The waterway remains a focal point of regional tensions, with Iran and Oman actively negotiating a temporary transit route.

U.S. President Donald Trump recently stated on social media that a ceasefire was imminent, while his administration seeks to keep petroleum flowing through the strait.

However, Iranian officials and international allies maintain that drifting mines remain a significant threat to navigation.

Ahead of the Thursday meeting, Qatari Foreign Ministry spokesperson Majed Al Ansari reiterated the need to restore the strait’s freedom of navigation to its pre-February 28 status quo to ensure unimpeded global maritime traffic.
