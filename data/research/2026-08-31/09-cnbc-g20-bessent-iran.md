This was CNBC's live blog of day one of the U.S. G20 finance ministerial in Asheville, North Carolina. Published Mon, Aug 31 2026 12:52 PM UTC.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh christened Monday's gathering of the Group of 20 finance ministers in Asheville, North Carolina, by singing the gospel of growth. "The world is awash in debt," Bessent told reporters before kicking off the two-day meeting, "and the only way for us to get out of this is to grow our way out of this."

Warsh, 100 days into his tenure, bade good riddance to "secular stagnation": "The new period is one of secular growth… one of a global investment surge."

## Iran-specific highlights (EDT timestamps)

**9:16 AM — Bessent: Iran is taking sanctions 'very seriously.'** Recent rhetorical and military aggression from Iran's leaders shows "they're in shock at the state of their economy." "I would think that they are lashing out kinetically because they are losing economically."

**9:25 AM — Bessent thanks EU for supporting 'Operation Economic Outcast.'** "They made it known today in the strongest terms that they supported" the plan. The European Commission said overnight in a statement it "welcomes efforts at ensuring that Iran ceases its destabilising activities and engages in peace negotiations with good faith" and supports that goal "through additional economic pressure, including through the US led Operation Economic Outcast." "The EU will continue to work closely with the United States and other G7 and international partners to maintain pressure on Iran and contribute to de-escalation and regional stability."

**9:30 AM — Bessent: Iran's economy could collapse 'within weeks or months.'** "We have the blockade, and we are going to continue exerting pressure, and we've had very good discussions here already. I think it could be within weeks or months" — but "The economy doesn't have to collapse ... we just have to have the regime come to their senses."

**10:29 AM — Bessent to push global counterparts on Iran.** Expected to ask counterparts in virtually every G20 meeting if their countries will join the US in cutting off Iran from the global economy. Confirmed he spoke with Chinese central bank Governor Pan Gongsheng on Sunday and will meet his Canadian counterpart. Per AP: "all options are on the table" for Beijing's continued Iranian oil purchases; plans to unveil sanctions on another bank this week.

**10:47 AM — Bessent: Sanctions pressure on Iran can work without Chinese help.** "I would push back on that false narrative that somehow the media has jumped on this, 'Oh, you can't do it without China.' Well, you can, because one of the things is there's only the 30 million of Iranian barrels of Iranian oil left on the water because of the blockade. So even if they were to get remittances from China, that's going to run out." China has publicly said it opposes "illicit unilateral sanctions that have no basis in international law." "We have more in common with the Chinese on Iran than we disagree on. The Chinese agree that Iran cannot have a nuclear weapon. The Chinese agree that the [Strait of Hormuz] must be open to free and fair ship transit."

**11:43 AM — Bessent meets Russia's finance chief.** Bessent discussed Trump's "peace plan" with Russian Finance Minister Anton Siluanov in Asheville, the Treasury Department said (photo of Bessent and Siluanov at the meeting).

**11:13 AM — Some reporters barred.** Treasury blocked reporters from The New York Times, The Wall Street Journal and Bloomberg from attending the Asheville meetings; nearly 300 members of the media, including one Times reporter, retain access, per a Treasury spokesperson: "News coverage should be focused on informing and educating the American people, rather than prioritizing clicks, engagement, or sensationalism."

**10:44 AM — U.S. CEOs attend G20 finance ministers for first time** (Goldman's Solomon, JPMorgan's Dimon, Truist's Lyons, Eli Lilly's Ricks, Deere's May, Medtronic's Martha, 3M's Brown), per a senior Treasury official.

Other: Bessent vs Druckenmiller over bond-market intervention; Bessent claims 10-year yield "flat" under Trump; Warsh "global investment surge" theme; G20 event includes CEOs for the first time.
