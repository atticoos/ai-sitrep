Published 16:57 (site local) — 2 September 2026 AD ـ 20 Rabi' Al-Awwal 1448 AH.

Qatar strongly condemned on Wednesday Iran's attack on the Saudi tanker SIDR in the Strait of Hormuz, which resulted in the deaths among the crew.

It slammed the strike as a blatant violation of international maritime law and UN Security Council Resolution 2817.

In a statement, the Qatari Ministry of Foreign Affairs "categorically rejected the weaponization of the Strait of Hormuz and demanded its unconditional reopening."

The ministry emphasized that "freedom of navigation in the vital waterway is a non-negotiable principle".

It warned that its continued closure jeopardizes regional security and the global economy.

Demanding an immediate halt to Iran's unjustified aggression, the ministry stressed Doha's "full solidarity with Saudi Arabia and expressed unequivocal support for all measures taken by Riyadh to protect its national assets."

Jordan also condemned the attack, saying it was a flagrant violation of international law and a direct threat to the security and safety of global maritime navigation.

In a statement, the Jordanian Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates underlined the country's "absolute solidarity with Saudi Arabia, expressing full support for all measures taken by Riyadh to protect its security, stability, and national resources."

Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) Secretary-General Jasem Albudaiwi strongly condemned the attack, slamming it as a "dangerous escalation, a grave violation of international law, and a direct threat to the security and safety of international maritime navigation."

He warned that the attack exposes regional security and stability and the flow of global trade to "grave risks".

Albudaiwi underscored that the GCC member states stand firmly with Saudi Arabia in every measure to safeguard its security and protect its interests.

### Multinational Maritime Defense Coalition Begins Operations from Saudi Headquarters (18:12, Jeddah)

The Multinational Maritime Defense Coalition has begun operations from its headquarters in Saudi Arabia, with Pakistan's Commodore Rashid Sheikh taking up his post as deputy commander.

Liaison officers from member states and countries completing accession procedures have also started work at the headquarters. Others will follow in stages, based on the number of posts allocated to each country.

The move follows the completion of arrangements to activate the coalition's bodies, approved at its third planning meeting in Jeddah in August and attended by representatives from 39 countries.

It marks the start of the coalition's institutional and operational phase and the integration of member states' military capabilities, while other countries finalize accession procedures.

Work on the coalition's institutional structure has also advanced rapidly. Saudi Arabia has appointed its commander, while members agreed that the first deputy commander would come from Pakistan.

Saudi Arabia announced the coalition in late July as an international defensive initiative open to all countries.

It aims to strengthen maritime security, protect international waterways and global trade, and counter emerging threats. Rear Admiral Abdullah Al-Shehri was appointed coalition commander.

The coalition is a strategic step toward protecting shared maritime interests in the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, Red Sea and Gulf of Aden.

It is founded on the principle that securing international waterways is a collective responsibility requiring coordinated action against growing threats.

Membership remains open to interested countries.

Saudi Arabia's hosting and leadership of the coalition reflects the Kingdom's role in strengthening regional and international security and building effective alliances to deter and confront shared maritime threats.

The coalition seeks to safeguard freedom of navigation, secure trade routes, supply chains and energy resources, and protect shared maritime interests across the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, Red Sea and Gulf of Aden.

The coalition operates under the principles of the United Nations Charter and international law. It is exclusively defensive, does not target any country or organization, fully respects state sovereignty and safeguards freedom of navigation.

### Riyadh Condemns Iran Attack on Saudi Vessel in Hormuz (12:48)

Saudi Arabia condemned on Wednesday Iran for attacking a Saudi vessel, SIDR, as it sailed the Hormuz Strait and the resulting loss of life among the crew.

A Foreign Ministry statement said the Kingdom also condemns the Iranian attacks against Jordan, Bahrain and Kuwait.

Saudi Arabia "stands by the fraternal states and the measures they take to protect their security and sovereignty against any threat," it added.

The Kingdom "underscored the need to stop the escalation and respect international marine lanes and their safety, as well as the global energy supplies."

It urged "good neighborliness and respect for the sovereignty of nations, the UN Charter and international laws and norms."

The Kingdom "called on all parties to restore calm, end the escalation, respect international law, return to the negotiations and turn to peaceful solutions."

### Saudi Shipping Company Bahri Says Two Filipino Sailors Killed in Incident in Hormuz (10:26)

Saudi Arabia's national shipping company, Bahri, said on Wednesday that two Filipino seafarers were killed in a security incident involving its vessel SIDR as it transited through the Strait of Hormuz on August 31.

The company said in a statement that it continues to maintain constant communication with the vessel and is coordinating with the relevant authorities while monitoring developments in the incident.

Bahri emphasized that the safety of its crew members, protection of the marine environment, and safe management of its vessels remain its top priorities, at a time when shipping traffic through the Strait of Hormuz is experiencing disruptions and escalating security risks.

SIDR was among two tankers involved in separate incidents occurring in close succession as they passed near the Strait of Hormuz, according to maritime sources.
