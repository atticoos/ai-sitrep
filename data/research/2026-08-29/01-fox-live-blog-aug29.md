## Iran says Hormuz deal with Oman reached but won't take effect until US honors Islamabad pact: report

Iran and Oman have reached an understanding over the Strait of Hormuz, but Tehran says it will not be implemented until the U.S. fulfills commitments under the Islamabad memorandum of understanding, according to Iran International English (iranintl.com/en/202608296391), citing Iranian media.

"The Strait of Hormuz remains closed, and any vessel passing through the strait does so in coordination with Iran," Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said Saturday, according to the outlet.

Gharibabadi said Tehran would continue what he called its "defensive measures" and is "prepared for any scenario." He also addressed recent visits by Qatari and Pakistani officials, saying their diplomatic efforts were aimed at determining whether the parties could return to implementing commitments made under the Islamabad MoU.

The claims come as Washington and Tehran remain at odds over control of the strategic waterway, with U.S. Central Command maintaining that Iran does not control the Strait of Hormuz. [Photo caption: FM Abbas Araghchi meets Omani FM Badr Al-Busaidi in Tehran, Aug 25, 2026.]

## Iran suffering from 'economic and livelihood challenges' as US economic campaign mounts

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei said "there is the need to seriously address the chain of economic and livelihood challenges, such as inflation, unemployment, management of prices and the market for goods and services," according to Reuters, as the U.S. economic campaign against Tehran is mounting.

The Treasury Department launched Operation Economic Outcast against Iran on Monday, and on Friday, revealed it is taking action against a United Arab Emirates bank in a bid to "cut off a key financial lifeline and sanctions evasion vehicle for the Iranian regime."

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said to state media that Iranian exports have dropped nearly 35% due to ongoing sanctions against Iran and a U.S. military blockade of Iranian ports, Reuters added. ## At least 6,000 sailors stranded by Iran war, UN maritime agency says

At least 6,000 sailors remain stranded aboard hundreds of ships in the Persian Gulf six months into the war with Iran, according to the United Nations' maritime agency. The International Maritime Organization said Friday that 19 seafarers have been killed since the conflict began Feb. 28. At least 70 attacks on international shipping have also been recorded in the Persian Gulf during the war, according to the agency.

## Threat level remains 'severe' for ships in the Strait of Hormuz, UK maritime agency says

The risk of danger that ships face while transiting the Strait of Hormuz remains heightened Saturday as vessel traffic has been "significantly reduced" during the U.S.-Iran war, according to the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Organization (UKMTO). The U.K. agency said this week that the "threat assessment remains elevated, with SEVERE risk assessed for the Strait of Hormuz and SUBSTANTIAL across the Gulf of Oman, Gulf of Aden, and Bab el Mandeb."

"Despite a brief recovery in late June, vessel traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remains significantly reduced, AIS transit information suggests operators increasingly favouring the Northern route following projectile attacks, ongoing security concerns, and heightened enforcement activity," read a report released by the UKMTO.

## UAE bank allegedly tied to Iranian networks is reviewing Treasury's claims with 'utmost seriousness'

A United Arab Emirates branch of an Egyptian bank that the Treasury Department says is linked to the Iranian regime said Saturday it is reviewing the accusations with "utmost seriousness and attention." The Treasury Department said Friday that "under Operation Economic Outcast, the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) proposed a rule that would revoke Banque Misr UAE's correspondent banking access to U.S. financial institutions." Treasury described the bank as a "critical node" for Tehran's access to American dollars: "Treasury estimates that between January 2024 and June 2026, Banque Misr UAE processed approximately $1.8 billion for 103 companies that are potentially part of Iranian shadow banking networks."

## Iran is ready to pursue dialogue with the US, diplomat says

Iranian ambassador to Germany Majid Nili said Tehran is ready to pursue dialogue again with the U.S., a report said. "Despite the existing political atmosphere and pressures, the path of dialogue and engagement based on mutual respect is still being pursued," Nili told Iran's IRNA News Agency, according to the Middle East Eye. The comment comes after Iranian Foreign Minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi said Friday that "putting diplomacy back on track isn't impossible" between the U.S. and Iran: "It hinges on U.S. understanding of one simple fact: pressure doesn't work. The U.S. should build trust, speak respectfully, acknowledge our rights, and uphold commitments," Araghchi wrote on X.

## Iranian general suggests its military has 'surprises' in store: report

Iran's acting defense minister Brig. Gen. Majid Ebn al-Reza suggested that Iran's military has "surprises" in store for its adversaries, a report said. "Everyone, and especially the enemies, should know that they will become aware of Iran's surprises in due time," he was quoted as saying by Iran's Tasnim News Agency. "We should not talk about the surprises now, but Iranian knowledge, the Iranian youth, and the things being carried out in the defense industries will, God willing, bring good surprises." The claims come after President Donald Trump said earlier this week that the Iranian regime is "not paying their troops" and is "in deep trouble" following the launch of the U.S. Treasury Department's Operation Economic Outcast against Tehran.

## Iranian president strikes defiant tone, says Tehran will 'stand strong against any aggressor'

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said Saturday that his country does not welcome war, "but we will stand strong against any aggressor, and no power can force a nation that stands with all its might and will to surrender," a report said. Pezeshkian spoke as the U.S.-Iran war passed the 6-month mark this week. "Despite the measures taken in the field of diplomacy and with the support of the people, the efforts of managers and statesmen, and the efforts of the commanders of the armed forces, we will not bow to any threat, as we have shown many times before," Pezeshkian added, according to the state-run IRIB News Agency.

## White House responds to reports Trump will meet oil refiners as Iran war pressures gas prices

The White House responded Saturday to reports that President Donald Trump will meet with U.S. oil refiners and fuel retailers next week as the administration faces pressure to bring down gasoline prices during the war with Iran. Reuters reported Thursday, citing people familiar with the matter, that Trump is expected to convene refiners and fuel retailers next week to discuss efforts to lower prices for consumers. Bloomberg separately reported that the meeting is planned for the afternoon of Tuesday, Sept. 1, with representatives from at least 10 fuel makers and distributors. Asked by Fox News about the reported meeting, a White House official did not confirm the gathering: "The Trump Administration will make a decision that is best for consumers, farmers, and energy supply chains. Any announcement will b[e made]…"

## Iran running on 'fewer and fewer dollars' as US pressure bites, expert warns collapse not guaranteed

U.S. sanctions and the blockade are increasingly squeezing Iran's access to hard currency, but economic pain alone may not be enough to topple the Islamic Republic, a prominent Israeli Iran expert said. "There is one thing that is undisputed. Iran has been forced in recent weeks to manage with fewer and fewer dollars," Tel Aviv University professor Uzi Rabi told Israel's 103FM radio, according to The Jerusalem Post (jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-906970). "When it lacks hard currency, it cannot even operate its agriculture and industry." Rabi called the U.S. blockade "theoretically the best thing to do" if Washington remains consistent, but cautioned against assuming severe economic distress will automatically bring down Tehran.
