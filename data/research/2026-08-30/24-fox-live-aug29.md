## Iran running on 'fewer and fewer dollars' as US pressure bites, expert warns collapse not guaranteed

U.S. sanctions and the blockade are increasingly squeezing Iran's access to hard currency, but economic pain alone may not be enough to topple the Islamic Republic, a prominent Israeli Iran expert said.

"There is one thing that is undisputed. Iran has been forced in recent weeks to manage with fewer and fewer dollars," Tel Aviv University professor Uzi Rabi told Israel's 103FM radio, according to The Jerusalem Post.

"When it lacks hard currency, it cannot even operate its agriculture and industry," said Rabi, who heads the university's Moshe Dayan Center for Middle Eastern and African Studies.

Rabi called the U.S. blockade "theoretically the best thing to do" if Washington remains consistent, but cautioned against assuming severe economic distress will automatically bring down Tehran. "Iran is a battered country," he said, while noting the regime still has alternative channels including smuggling and cryptocurrency.

## Iran says Hormuz deal with Oman reached but won't take effect until US honors Islamabad pact: report

Iran and Oman have reached an understanding over the Strait of Hormuz, but Tehran says it will not be implemented until the U.S. fulfills commitments under the Islamabad memorandum of understanding, according to Iran International English (iranintl.com/en/202608296391), citing Iranian media.

"The Strait of Hormuz remains closed, and any vessel passing through the strait does so in coordination with Iran," Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said Saturday, according to the outlet.

Gharibabadi said Tehran would continue what he called its "defensive measures" and is "prepared for any scenario."

The Iranian official also addressed recent visits by Qatari and Pakistani officials, saying their diplomatic efforts were aimed at determining whether the parties could return to implementing commitments made under the Islamabad MoU.

The claims come as Washington and Tehran remain at odds over control of the strategic waterway, with U.S. Central Command maintaining that Iran does not control the Strait of Hormuz. [Posted by Jasmine Baehr]

## US economic offensive zeroes in on Dubai as Iran's critical financial lifeline: report

The Trump administration's expanding economic campaign against Iran is increasingly targeting financial and trade channels through Dubai, Istanbul and Baghdad rather than focusing first on Beijing, according to an analysis by Iran International English (iranintl.com/en/202608288051).

The outlet reported that Tehran needs hard currency it can readily spend to defend the rial, pay military and security forces and keep salaries and subsidies flowing, while much of the money Iran earns selling oil to China remains held in yuan.

## Threat level remains 'severe' for ships in the Strait of Hormuz, UK maritime agency says

The risk of danger that ships face while transiting the Strait of Hormuz remains heightened Saturday as vessel traffic has been "significantly reduced" during the U.S.-Iran war, according to the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Organization (UKMTO).

The U.K. agency said this week that the "threat assessment remains elevated, with SEVERE risk assessed for the Strait of Hormuz and SUBSTANTIAL across the Gulf of Oman, Gulf of Aden, and Bab el Mandeb."

"Despite a brief recovery in late June, vessel traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remains significantly reduced, AIS transit information suggests operators increasingly favouring the Northern route following projectile attacks, ongoing security concerns, and heightened enforcement activity," read a report released by the UKMTO. [Posted by Greg Norman-Diamond]

## Iran is ready to pursue dialogue with the US, diplomat says

Iranian ambassador to Germany Majid Nili said Tehran is ready to pursue dialogue again with the U.S., a report said.

"Despite the existing political atmosphere and pressures, the path of dialogue and engagement based on mutual respect is still being pursued," Nili told Iran's IRNA News Agency, according to the Middle East Eye.

The comment comes after Iranian Foreign Minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi said Friday that "putting diplomacy back on track isn't impossible" between the U.S. and Iran. "It hinges on U.S. understanding of one simple fact: pressure doesn't work," Araghchi wrote on X. "The U.S. should build trust, speak respectfully, acknowledge our rights, and uphold commitments." ## UAE bank allegedly tied to Iranian networks is reviewing Treasury's claims with 'utmost seriousness'

A United Arab Emirates branch of an Egyptian bank that the Treasury Department says is linked to the Iranian regime said Saturday it is reviewing the accusations with "utmost seriousness and attention."

The Treasury Department said Friday that "under Operation Economic Outcast, the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) proposed a rule that would revoke Banque Misr UAE's correspondent banking access to U.S. financial institutions." Treasury described the bank as a "critical node" for Tehran's access to American dollars.

"Treasury estimates that between January 2024 and June 2026, Banque Misr UAE processed approximately $1.8 billion for 103 companies that are potentially part of Iranian shadow banking networks," it said. "Because Iran is already subject to comprehensive U.S. sanctions, in order to generate revenue abroad, it relies on multi-jurisdictional shadow banking networks that provide key access to U.S. dollar correspondent banking relationships. Iran uses these shadow banking networks to launder funds, procure weapons, and bankroll its regional terrorist proxy groups."

Banque Misr said in a statement Saturday that it "affirms its full respect for the relevant regulatory and legal frameworks, its adherence to all applicable laws, regulations, instructions and supervisory controls, and its full and constructive cooperation with the relevant authorities."

"Within the framework of Banque Misr's full respect for the applicable regulatory process, the Bank clarifies that what has been announced constitutes a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM), which is subject to a formal period for receiving and reviewing comments before any final rule is issued," it added. "The Bank is treating the NPRM and the information and assessments contained therein with the utmost seriousness and attention and is reviewing it thoroughly. The Bank will communicate with the U.S. Department of the Treasury to obtain further information and maintain ongoing cooperation, in preparation for taking the necessary legal and regulatory actions and submitting its response to the relevant authorities within the specified period." [Posted by Greg Norman-Diamond]

## IRGC Navy claims 'completely decisive' control of Strait of Hormuz, rips US 'obvious lie': report

The Navy of the Islamic Revolution Guards Corps (IRGC) argued Saturday that it has full control over the Strait of Hormuz, according to a report.

"The statements by U.S. officials regarding the Strait of Hormuz being open are an obvious lie and are solely intended to control the price of oil and cover up their failures," the IRGC Navy was quoted as saying by Iran's Tasnim News Agency. "The control of the warriors of Islam over this strategic waterway is completely decisive."

U.S. Central Command, which has been enforcing a blockade of Iranian ports, told Fox News Digital earlier this week that "Iran does not control the Strait of Hormuz." "The strait is an international waterway that commercial vessels continue to transit with U.S. military assistance," CENTCOM spokesperson Capt. Tim Hawkins said. [Posted by Greg Norman-Diamond]

## Iranian president strikes defiant tone, says Tehran will 'stand strong against any aggressor'

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said Saturday that his country does not welcome war, "but we will stand strong against any aggressor, and no power can force a nation that stands with all its might and will to surrender," a report said. Pezeshkian spoke as the U.S.-Iran war passed the 6-month mark this week.

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei revealed that the country is dealing with economic problems as the U.S. Treasury Department's Operation Economic Outcast is widening, Reuters reported. [Posted by Greg Norman-Diamond]

## Iran suffering from 'economic and livelihood challenges' as US economic campaign mounts

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei said "there is the need to seriously address the chain of economic and livelihood challenges, such as inflation, unemployment, management of prices and the market for goods and services," according to Reuters, as the U.S. economic campaign against Tehran is mounting.

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said to state media that Iranian exports have dropped nearly 35% due to ongoing sanctions against Iran and a U.S. military blockade of Iranian ports, Reuters added (reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/war-weighs-irans-economy-us-intensifies-sanctions-2026-08-29/). [Posted by Greg Norman-Diamond]

## At least 6,000 sailors stranded by Iran war, UN maritime agency says

At least 6,000 sailors remain stranded aboard hundreds of ships in the Persian Gulf six months into the war with Iran, according to the United Nations' maritime agency.

The International Maritime Organization said Friday that 19 seafarers have been killed since the conflict began Feb. 28. At least 70 attacks on international shipping have also been recorded in the Persian Gulf during the war, according to the agency.

The disruption comes as shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains well below normal levels despite U.S. efforts to secure the critical waterway. Before the war, roughly 20% of the world's traded oil and natural gas passed through the Strait of Hormuz. [Posted by Brittany Miller; AP contributed]
