## Trump warns US or Europe would be 'next' if Iran gets nuclear weapon

President Donald Trump said Sunday that Iran would have used a nuclear weapon if it had one, warning that Israel and much of the Middle East could have been destroyed, with the United States or Europe possibly "next." "If they would have had a nuclear weapon, they would have fired it," Trump said on "Sunday Night in America." "These are very violent, evil people." "So I think the Middle East would have essentially been gone, but Israel definitely would have been gone," he added. "I guess we would be next, or Europe, or someplace. But no, you cannot let them have a nuclear weapon, and they won't have a nuclear weapon," he added.

Trump also praised the success of the blockade of Iranian ports and Washington's financial pressure campaign, saying Iran had become "a failing nation." "The blockade has been unbelievable," he said. "The financial hits that we're giving them" have contributed to Iran's decline. "Their navy's gone. Their air force is gone. Their leaders are gone. All of their anti-aircraft — I mean, it's all wiped out. They're tough people," Trump said. The president also said he was surprised when Tehran began targeting Saudi Arabia, Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain and Kuwait at the start of the war, but argued the attacks eroded Iran's regional support. "Everybody was shocked. I was surprised," Trump said. "I thought it was a real good thing because they lost all the support that they had."

## CENTCOM rejects Iran's 'act of aggression' claim, says US eliminated Hormuz threat

U.S. Central Command on Sunday rejected Iran's claim that U.S. strikes aimed at preventing the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps from laying mines in the Strait of Hormuz were an "act of aggression." CENTCOM called the accusation "false," in a post shared on X (x.com/CENTCOM/status/2094228119079067852, timestamped 12:57 AM · Aug 31, 2026) and said U.S. forces took "limited, precise action" against IRGC minelaying forces that posed an imminent threat to the strategic waterway. "In essence, Iran created the threat and the U.S. military eliminated it to protect civilian mariners, commercial shipping, and the free flow of global commerce," CENTCOM said.

## US to hit Iran with new sanctions every week as Bessent puts global banks on notice

The U.S. Treasury plans to roll out new sanctions targeting Iran on a weekly basis, beginning with banks that help Tehran access the global financial system, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Reuters on Sunday (reuters.com/business/finance/bessent-expects-new-us-secondary-sanctions-weekly-aiming-increase-pressure-iran-2026-08-30/). "You're going to see a lot more of these every week," Bessent told the outlet ahead of a G20 finance leaders meeting in North Carolina. "We're starting with the banks, and we're telling the banks it's not okay to have Iranian money and to aid the regime." Bessent said the administration could go as far as cutting a financial institution off entirely from the U.S. dollar-based financial system. His comments came after the Treasury Department imposed penalties Friday on the United Arab Emirates branches of Egypt's Banque Misr over alleged financial links to Iran.

## US struck Iranian launchers after intel uncovered plot to fire mine-carrying rockets into Hormuz

U.S. forces struck Iranian missile launchers on Larak Island on Sunday after intelligence indicated Tehran was preparing to fire rockets carrying sea mines into the Strait of Hormuz, Fox News chief foreign correspondent Trey Yingst reported. The plan to launch rockets with sea mines was designed to redeploy the explosives in the strait after U.S. forces demined the area, he said on "The Big Weekend Show." President Donald Trump had said Aug. 25 that the U.S. Navy had informed him that all mines had been removed from the strait's waters. Trump also warned Iran that any vessel caught laying new ones would be "immediately and systematically destroyed." The developments came as U.S. forces in Jordan faced missile attacks Sunday in retaliation for the strikes on Larak Island. Yingst said American forces stationed in Jordan had multiple air-defense systems to counter such threats, including Patriot and THAAD missile-defense systems deployed in the region. "At this point, there are no significant impacts, and nearly all of the incoming missiles have been intercepted," Yingst said. "These are serious attacks," he added.

## Jordan intercepts 8 Iranian missiles after US forces strike launchers

Jordan's Armed Forces confirmed Sunday that its military had intercepted eight missiles fired from Iran that entered Jordanian airspace on Sunday, Reuters reported, citing state TV. The announcement came after U.S. forces struck two Iranian missile launchers on Larak Island on Sunday, U.S. Central Command spokesman Capt. Tim Hawkins told Fox News.

## IRGC claims combined missile, drone attacks on two US air bases in Jordan

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) claimed Sunday that it had launched a combined ballistic missile and drone attack on two U.S. air bases in Jordan in response to the U.S. strike on Iran's Larak Island. Citing a statement carried by Tasnim News, Iran International reported that the IRGC said it targeted technical and maintenance infrastructure, as well as areas where fighter jets were stationed, also claiming to have inflicted "heavy damage." Reuters reported that Iranian media claimed the targets were King Hussein Air Base and Al-Azraq Air Base in Jordan.

## Trump taps world's largest oil reserves as Iran war rattles energy markets

President Donald Trump's new oil deal with Venezuela could give the U.S. another source of crude as the war with Iran continues to disrupt global energy markets. Trump announced the U.S. had secured majority control of more than 65 billion barrels of Venezuela's proven oil reserves through a partnership with private businesses. The agreement comes as the conflict with Iran has sharply curtailed oil shipments through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint that handled roughly 20% of global petroleum flows before the war. The disruption has helped push U.S. gasoline prices above $4 a gallon, while the nation's Strategic Petroleum Reserve has fallen below 300 million barrels. But the impact will not be immediate: Venezuela's oil infrastructure has deteriorated after years of underinvestment, and significantly increasing production could take years.

## Iran vows response to US attack on Larak Island

Iran's Revolutionary Guards (IRGC) claimed Sunday that the U.S. strike on Larak Island killed and wounded several Iranian fighters and civilians. The guards also vowed to respond and punish those responsible, state broadcaster IRIB reported, according to Reuters. The IRGC did not provide a precise casualty toll, Reuters reported. "The American-Zionist adversary, once more acting out of frustration over its domestic challenges and waning influence in the region, aims to resurrect its malevolent presence and sway public perception," the statement read.

## Breaking: US forces strike Iranian missile launchers on Larak Island

U.S. forces struck two Iranian missile launchers on Larak Island earlier Sunday, CENTCOM spokesman Capt. Tim Hawkins told Fox News. Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) forces were observed preparing to launch rockets and deploy sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz, Hawkins said.
