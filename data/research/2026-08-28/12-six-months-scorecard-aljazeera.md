[Al Jazeera feature, published Friday 28 Aug 2026. Six-month war scorecard — key extracted sections:]

In the months since [the war began], the war has erupted on several other fronts, reaching as far as the Caspian Sea. It has triggered a global oil supply crisis as the US and Iran battle for control of the strategic Strait of Hormuz, through which more than one-fifth of oil and natural gas cargo transited. And it has resulted in an estimated 8,000-10,000 deaths, as well as tens of thousands of injuries.

The conflict is also realigning the global order, experts say, as new alliances form and dominant ones show signs of crumbling.

## Has the US achieved any of its war objectives?

Iran's nuclear programme: Despite Trump's claim that the US had "obliterated" Iran's nuclear capabilities, repeated strikes have likely caused vast damage to the facilities, but it is believed Tehran still possesses 440kg of highly enriched uranium, which could be used to make a weapon. The Trump administration wanted Iran to hand over this stock and commit to zero enrichment; Iran refused, though at one point appeared open to handing the stock to a third country. In the June 17 memorandum of understanding (MoU), Iran agreed not to pursue a nuclear weapon. The MoU unravelled as its vague wording led to disagreements about what it actually stipulated.

Regime change: On day one of the war — February 28 — the US killed [Ayatollah Ali] Khamenei alongside several other senior officials. Iran's political leadership has, however, remained largely the same under the new Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei — the late ayatollah's son. "Killing leaders does not mean strategic achievements, even if it's a tactical success," said Saleh [ANU professor Alam Saleh].

Ending support for regional proxy groups: No signs Tehran's support has ended; the demand did not appear in the MoU. Hezbollah went to war with Israel in Iran's defence, and Iran has refused to negotiate an end to the war unless the Lebanon front is included. The Houthis have launched a blockade on Saudi Arabia in the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, partly in support of Iran.

Iran's ballistic missile programme: Trump has claimed about 82 percent of Iran's missiles are gone and the navy is degraded; in April he said 158 Iranian naval vessels had been hit. Iran, however, has continued to fire missiles, most recently a long-range missile in July that killed three US soldiers in Jordan. The demand for an end to the missile programme was absent from the July MoU.

Control of Iranian oil: In June, Trump said the US would seize and control Iran's Kharg island. To date, the US does not control any Iranian territory.
