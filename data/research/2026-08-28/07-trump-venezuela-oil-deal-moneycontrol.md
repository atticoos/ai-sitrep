US President Donald Trump on Friday announced what he called the "biggest oil deal in world history" with Venezuela, saying the United States has secured majority control over oil reserves covering more than 65 billion barrels.

The agreement involves 17 strategic Venezuelan oil fields and could bring nearly $100 billion in private investment to the country, while generating more than $209 billion in tax revenue for the Venezuelan state, according to officials.

"The United States of America has just entered into an Agreement with the Country of Venezuela on, THE BIGGEST OIL DEAL IN WORLD HISTORY!" Trump wrote on Truth Social.

Trump said the agreement was negotiated by US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth with Venezuela's interim President Delcy Rodriguez.

## US To Get 55% Effective Output

Under the agreement, a new private company involving the US and an unnamed operator will develop the 17 Venezuelan oil fields, according to a US official familiar with the arrangement. The United States will receive a 55% effective share of the new company's output, including an ownership stake and the right to purchase oil at cost, the Associated Press reported. Venezuela has also granted the company 100-year rights to develop the fields.

Oil purchased from the company would be used to help replenish the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve and for military needs, according to the US official cited by AP.

Trump said the transaction would come at no cost to US taxpayers. He also claimed it would more than double US oil reserves, increase supplies and substantially lower gasoline prices for American consumers.

The 65 billion barrels cited in the agreement are associated with the 17 fields covered by the deal. The agreement does not mean the US will immediately produce or receive 65 billion barrels of crude.

## Venezuela Expects $209 Billion In Tax Revenue

Rubio said the agreement would bring nearly $100 billion in private investment to Venezuela, create thousands of jobs and support reconstruction of the country's economy. Rodriguez said the agreement could generate more than $209 billion in tax revenue for the Venezuelan state.

Venezuela has the world's largest proven crude oil reserves, estimated at about 303 billion barrels, according to the US Energy Information Administration. The country accounts for about 17% of global reserves. Despite its vast reserves, Venezuela produces only about 1% of the world's oil. Years of underinvestment and severely damaged infrastructure have limited its ability to increase production.

The 17 fields covered by the agreement are located mainly in the Orinoco Belt and Lake Maracaibo regions, Reuters reported.

## Deal Faces Infrastructure, Legal Hurdles

Reviving Venezuela's oil industry will require major investment in wells, pipelines, processing facilities and transportation infrastructure. Much of the country's oil infrastructure has deteriorated after years of underinvestment.

The agreement could also face legal challenges in Venezuela. Reuters reported that analysts had questioned whether the proposed arrangement would comply with the country's constitution and hydrocarbons law, given the state's longstanding control over the oil industry. US oil companies have also historically been cautious about returning to Venezuela because of political instability, damaged infrastructure and the country's history of nationalising foreign-owned assets.

## Trump Pushes Venezuelan Oil Amid High Fuel Prices

The agreement comes as the Trump administration seeks additional sources of crude amid disruptions to global oil supplies and elevated US gasoline prices. The US has drawn heavily on its Strategic Petroleum Reserve this year, while the war with Iran has sharply reduced oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz, a major global energy chokepoint.

The average US gasoline price stood at about $4.09 a gallon on Friday, compared with $3.21 a year earlier, according to AAA.

Trump has argued that greater access to Venezuelan crude will increase US oil supplies and lower gasoline prices for American consumers. However, the impact will depend on how quickly investment reaches the fields and Venezuela's damaged oil infrastructure can be restored.
