US President Donald Trump has said the Strait of Hormuz is effectively open, arguing that Iran has lost much of its leverage over the crucial energy chokepoint as US forces continue mine-clearing operations and Washington maintains pressure on Iranian ports.

"That sucker is open. The Iranian response is very mild. They don't want us to go back at them. That's the whole ball game. The rest doesn't matter," Trump told Axios in a phone interview on Thursday.

Trump's comments came after the US military said it had cleared internationally recognised shipping routes in the strait of Iranian sea mines.

## US says shipping routes are free of Iranian mines

US Central Command chief Adm. Brad Cooper said American forces had achieved a "major milestone" by clearing Iranian sea mines from internationally recognised shipping routes in the Strait of Hormuz.

"Bottom line: Today international shipping lanes are open, and momentum is building," Cooper said in a video released Thursday.

Cooper said US Navy divers, SEALs and aircraft had spent months working to clear the routes. The US says more than 200 mine-like objects have been removed from the main shipping lane, although only 11 were confirmed to be actual mines.

Trump had earlier said US forces had removed or detonated mines in international waters. However, the extent of the mine-clearing operation remains disputed. People familiar with US internal assessments told Bloomberg that American forces may have made significant progress but may not have cleared all of the estimated 80 to 150 mines believed to have been laid by Iran. US officials have rejected that assessment, saying those figures were outdated.

Axios reported that US officials believe Washington has steadily eroded Iran's ability to use the strait as leverage since the conflict began.

## Tanker traffic remains below pre-war levels

US officials estimate that 20 to 30 tankers are now travelling through the southern channel of the Strait of Hormuz each night, carrying around 9 million to 10 million barrels of oil. That is roughly half the level recorded before the war.

Washington is seeking to increase traffic further, with a target of allowing at least 50 ships to move through the waterway each night by mid-September. The US ultimately hopes to restore between 60% and 70% of pre-war oil exports.
