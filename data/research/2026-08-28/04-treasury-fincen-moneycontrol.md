The US Treasury on Friday proposed restricting Banque Misr UAE's access to the American financial system as Washington intensifies its campaign to economically isolate Iran.

The Treasury Department's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) issued a notice of proposed rulemaking that would prohibit US financial institutions from opening or maintaining correspondent accounts for, or on behalf of, Banque Misr UAE.

The proposal is part of the Trump administration's Operation Economic Outcast, which Washington says is aimed at disrupting financial networks that help Iran generate revenue and evade US sanctions.

## Why is Banque Misr UAE being targeted?

The Treasury described Banque Misr UAE as a "critical node" in Iran's access to US dollars and the international financial system.

According to Treasury estimates, Banque Misr UAE processed approximately $1.8 billion between January 2024 and June 2026 in transactions involving 103 companies that the department said were potentially part of Iranian shadow-banking networks.

Treasury also alleged that some of the bank's customers included apparent front companies used by Iran's Ministry of Defense and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) to evade US sanctions. The department further alleged that some of the entities were used to launder money on behalf of Iranian Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei.

These are allegations made by the US Treasury.

The proposed action applies specifically to Banque Misr UAE and does not target Banque Misr's operations in other countries.

## Treasury warns Iran's financial enablers

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Washington had promised to sever what it described as the remaining economic lifelines available to Iran, describing the move as the first step towards holding the bank accountable for what Treasury called its "continued, egregious support" of the Iranian regime.

The warning comes as Washington expands efforts to pressure foreign financial institutions that it says are helping Iran maintain access to international banking channels.

## What would the proposed rule do?

If adopted, the FinCEN rule would prohibit US financial institutions from opening or maintaining correspondent accounts for Banque Misr UAE. US financial institutions would also have to take reasonable steps to prevent transactions involving Banque Misr UAE from being processed through correspondent accounts held in the United States.

The measure is not yet a final prohibition and would take effect only if FinCEN finalises the proposed rule. Reuters reported that the proposed rule would revoke Banque Misr UAE's correspondent banking access to US financial institutions if finalised.

## US sanctions other Iran-linked financial facilitators

Alongside the proposed action against Banque Misr UAE, the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) announced sanctions against Reza Mohammad Taeedi, the manager of Iran's Bank Melli branch in Dubai. Treasury said Bank Melli had facilitated billions of dollars in transactions through accounts controlled by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Qods Force, allowing the group to move funds inside and outside Iran.

OFAC also sanctioned Hong Kong-based Kameng Trading Limited, which Treasury accused of helping a sanctioned Iranian exchange house launder money and access the international financial system.

The sanctions against Taeedi and Kameng Trading are separate from FinCEN's proposed action against Banque Misr UAE.

## Operation Economic Outcast

The latest measures come as the Trump administration expands its economic pressure campaign against Iran. Bessent launched Operation Economic Outcast earlier this week, warning countries and financial institutions that maintaining significant economic ties with Tehran could expose them to US sanctions.

Washington has said the campaign is aimed at targeting Iran's oil revenues, sanctions-evasion networks and financial intermediaries. Treasury has said entities that facilitate money laundering or sanctions evasion on behalf of Iran risk being cut off from the US financial system.

## Why US banking access matters

Correspondent banking relationships allow banks outside the United States to access the US financial system and facilitate international transactions, including dollar payments. If the proposed rule is finalised, Banque Misr UAE would lose its ability to maintain correspondent banking relationships with US financial institutions, potentially making dollar transactions and other international payments more difficult for the UAE operation.

The move marks another step in Washington's effort to pressure Iran by targeting not only Iranian entities but also foreign financial institutions that Treasury says facilitate transactions linked to Tehran.
